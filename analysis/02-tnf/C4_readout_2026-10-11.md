**3 of 6 pass the two-oracle pose gate.** The pre-registered prediction — rows 1–2 fail, rows 3–6
pass — was **right on 5 of 6 rows and missed row 6.**

## The split is by lineage, not by score

Rows 1 and 2 are lineage L1. Row 6 is the arm-H rebuild. Rows 3, 4 and 5 are all one L0 scaffold.
Phase 0 had already seen this on the round-3 *parents*: L0's checkpoint agreed across oracles
(0.5813 vs 0.6037) and L1's did not (0.0214 / 34.1 Å vs 0.6148 / 1.789 Å). **The disagreement
propagated from parent to children**, which is what makes it look like a property of the backbone
family rather than a bad draw.

🔴 **The two designs ranked FIRST and SECOND are among the three that fail**, and they are the two
with the highest ESMFold2 confidence in the whole set — median sc_DockQ 0.7497 and 0.7928, better
than anything the L0 scaffold produces. On one oracle they are the best molecules here. On the
other they are not bound to the target at all.

**This is not ESMFold2 seed noise.** Its lrms spans 1.01–6.65 Å over 17 folds for row 1 and
1.13–2.42 Å over 22 folds for row 2. It is consistently confident. Row 4's n is only 5 because its
human folds were written to `biomodals/runs/` by the relative-`OUT_DIR` bug, and they are the
complete arm-M set.

## What this is worth, stated against itself

- **One Chai-1 sample per design, seed 42, no MSA.** A single diffusion draw is the weakest version
  of this test. A 5-sample consensus or a second seed could move any row, and we did not buy it.
- **It does not reproduce selection**, as the reviewer said plainly. The corrective rounds-4–5
  re-run, which would have, stopped on its own cost ceiling. This is the robustness check he called
  useful, nothing more.
- **Two structure predictors agreeing does not establish binding**, so rows 3–5 passing is not
  evidence that they bind. It removes one failure mode: one oracle agreeing with itself.
- **The backbone mapping is validated by ESMFold2's own agreement.** Scoring a design against the
  wrong Genie 3 backbone would make BOTH oracles fail; ESMFold2 sits at 1.7–4.7 Å against the
  assigned backbone on every row, including row 6. So the Chai-1 failures are disagreements, not
  bookkeeping errors.

## The ranking does not change, as pre-registered

The pre-registration fixed this before folding: *"if some designs pass and others fail, the ranking
does not change … Re-ranking on a quantity measured after the fact, on n=1 sample per design, is
exactly the overfitting the breached guard existed to prevent. A failing design stays in its
current row with the failure stated next to it."* That holds. Rows are ranked on worse-of-two-species
margin — the one instrument with an external calibration — and this table goes into the submission
beside the ranking rather than reordering it.

**One coincidence worth naming without acting on it:** the three rows that pass C4a are rows 3, 4
and 5, which are also the three that must be tested together or not at all, being the 0/1/4
buried-histidine series on one scaffold. Those two facts have nothing to do with each other and we
are not going to pretend the series was chosen for this reason.

🔴 **Consequence for the earlier FIELD 2 wording.** Before C4 ran, the submission offered
"drop all three and test rows 1, 2 and 6" as the alternative to testing the series. Rows 1, 2 and 6
are now exactly the three that fail the second oracle, so that fallback cannot stand as written and
is being corrected rather than quietly left in.

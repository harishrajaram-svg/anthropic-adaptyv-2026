RESULT: BOTH BARS FAIL. 0 of 12 clear B1, 0 of 12 clear B3. The arm is a negative.

But it is a quantitative negative in two directions, and the second one is the finding.

1. THE REPAIR HYPOTHESIS WAS DIRECTIONALLY RIGHT AND UNDER-POWERED. The template relD50H sits at
   +4.097. The best rebuild reaches +5.433 -- a gain of +1.336, about 59% of the +2.25 deficit,
   from changing two to five residues. Local repacking DOES recover binding lost to a histidine
   substitution. It recovered more than half of it here and still fell 0.91 short of the floor.
   A consistent repair motif appears in every recovering design: K53 -> D or N, and L76 -> R.

2. THE REPAIR THAT RECOVERS BINDING STABILISES THE WRONG GEOMETRY. The template's His50 was
   unstable across seeds (4.39 / 7.94 / 15.68 / 16.36 / 14.00 A to the nearest cation). After the
   rebuild the pose is MORE stable -- design-side PAE improves from ~10.6 to 9.05-9.65 -- and it
   stabilises with His50 facing a target CARBOXYLATE in nearly every seed: the six best designs
   read tightening 0/5, 0/5, 1/5, 0/5, 0/5, 0/5 on the releasing call.
   Checked and NOT supported: that MPNN achieved this by introducing acids near the histidine.
   Spearman(margin recovered, new acids placed in the shell) = +0.063 over 12 designs. The acids
   are not the mechanism; the pose the optimiser found is.

3. ARM B CONFIRMS THE COST OF THE CLEAN MECHANISM. Removing the tightening His61 costs 7.1 units
   (A3's ctrlH61Q) and the shell cannot pay it back: arm B's best is +1.819 against arm A's +5.433.
   The only molecule in which the mechanism is unambiguous is the one that does not bind.

=> Objective 2 closes for the SIXTH time, and C7 is the first closure that explains itself at the
residue level: on this target, recovering the binding a histidine costs and keeping that histidine
pointed at a cation are competing objectives in the same shell. That is consistent with C6 (48
designs carry the geometry, none holds above +5.05) and with C3 (the margin is carried by Arg27,
Thr54 and Tyr20 -- none of them titratable).

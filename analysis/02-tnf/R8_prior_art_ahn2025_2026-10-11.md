# R8 — a published buried-His-network TNF-α binder switches 79x, and it is arm M's mechanism

## The result, precisely

**K_D 7.0 nM at pH 7.4 → 548 nM at pH 5.4**, SPR (Biacore 8K, McIlvaine's + 0.05% Tween20, 1:1
Langmuir). A **K_D ratio, not a rate ratio.** Target: Acro **TNA-H82E3**, biotinylated human TNF-α
trimer — the same vendor family as our **TNA-H4211**. A second His–Arg design gives 6.4 → 195 nM
(~30x). Campaign: 72 designs, 48 retained binding, 7 under 25 nM, **3 pH-sensitive**.

Posted **2025-09-29**, so it is prior art relative to everything in this project.

## 🔴 It is arm M's mechanism, independently and experimentally

The 79x does **not** come from epitope engineering. It comes from a **buried His–His network in the
binder's own hydrophobic core**, deliberately leaving the interface untouched — their stated reason
being that remodelling a working interface would cost affinity and cross-species reactivity.

That is **exactly** what arm M did today: `3_L0r4k0_quadH`, four buried histidines
(V45H/L53H/L63H/L64H), **+3.064 kcal/mol** of predicted folded-state destabilisation at pH 6.0, with
the interface left alone and binding preserved (+7.131 human, +7.185 mouse). We arrived at the same
mechanism on the same target independently, and **their experiment says it works at 79x.**

Three further convergences:
- **Naive histidine enrichment failed for them too** — global His bias gave "little pH-sensitivity"
  (EphA2 453→491 nM, PCSK9 13→16 nM). Our matching result: forcing His at interface positions
  collapsed the pose, 0.6349 → 0.0126.
- They select on **H-bond energy and burial depth** (`AtomicDepth`). Our C9e scan found the same
  axis, Spearman(burial, pKa) = **−0.600**.
- Their pipeline (a) uses **`dddG_elec`**, the Rosetta ddG recomputed on `fa_elec` alone at pH 5 vs 7.
  `bin/dddg_elec.py` in this repo is a reimplementation of that functional form, written before we
  knew of this paper.

## 🔴 What they do NOT have, and we do

**They perform no pKa or protonation calculation in the pipeline that produced the 79x binder.** pH
response is **by construction** — place a buried His H-bonded to a cation and assume protonation
repels. The Rosetta pH module, their ad hoc "pH score" and `dddG_elec` all belong to their *other*
pipeline, used for EphA2 and TNFR2, not TNF-α. There is **no pKa prediction anywhere in the paper**.

And their Discussion names the gap verbatim: *"Finer control over pH thresholds — particularly
optimizing for better responsiveness around pH 6.5 by shifting the pKa of histidine through tuning
of the surrounding residues — will be important."* **Future work, by their own account.**

That is precisely what this project measured: PROPKA on the buried histidines (singles 4.26–5.03;
the quad **2.43–4.55**, deepening on combination to 102% of additivity), the per-histidine
destabilisation **saturating at +0.775 kcal/mol**, and therefore the conclusion that the lever is
**count, not burial depth**. None of that is anticipated here.

## 🔴 And the pH window is the catch

**Their acidic arm is 5.4. The competition assay is 6.0.** Of their buried-network designs only
Neo2 was tested at 7.4/6.0 — and it is their **weakest** switch, stated as ">2-fold". So a 79x at
5.4 does not establish anything at 6.0, which is the same wall this project hit from the other side.

## What does not collide

- **Epitope.** They never state one. "Protomer", "trimer" and "seam" appear **zero** times; "His73"
  and "Glu146" **zero** times. No experimental structure of anything in the paper — all AF2 models,
  no PDB depositions. Our seam / His73 / Glu146 work is not anticipated.
- **Sequences.** **None released**, with a provisional patent pending. Sequence-level overlap cannot
  be checked in either direction, so no claim about it can be made.
- The parent of their TNF-α binder is **never named or cited**, and TNF-α is excluded from their
  own Methods list of de novo targets — so its provenance is a gap in the paper, not a match to ours.

## Consequences for the submission

1. **FIELD 2 must cite this paper.** A methodology write-up on designed pH-dependent TNF-α binders
   that omits the published 79x result on the same target would be a serious omission. It also
   *helps* the argument: it is independent evidence that the buried-core mechanism works here.
2. **It strengthens the case for the quad** as the design most aligned with an experimentally
   validated mechanism on this target — against the quad's 0.9518 identity to its own parent, which
   is the live objection.
3. **It weakens any novelty claim framed on mechanism alone.** The novelty argument has to rest on
   the **epitope** and on the **pKa/energetic measurement** they explicitly lack — not on the target,
   not on the buried-His idea, and not on fold-change magnitude.

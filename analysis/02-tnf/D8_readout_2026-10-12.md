## 🔴 D8c FAILED. Every design has a catastrophic sample, including all three controls.

Row 3 throws a 42.05 Å sample, row 4 throws 23.22 and 40.47, row 5 throws 41.67. These are the
designs where Chai-1 and ESMFold2 *agreed* in C4. **Chai-1 at one diffusion sample does not place
binders on this system reliably**, and C4 called three designs misplaced on exactly one draw each.

Per the pre-registration's outcome 3, fixed before any of this existed: **C4 is withdrawn as
evidence about the designs and becomes a statement about the predictor.** The submitted text changes
accordingly. This was the outcome I called least likely when I wrote the prereg, which is the whole
argument for having put the control in.

**I was wrong in the direction that matters.** I told Harish a 5-sample consensus would turn C4 from
suggestive into decisive. It dissolved it. The single draw also *overstated* the distances it
reported: rows 1 and 2 came in at 26.1 and 35.9 Å in C4 against sampled medians of 19.2 and 19.3.

## What survives, stated as narrowly as it deserves

The in-gate counts are **0/5, 0/5, 4/5, 3/5, 4/5, 2/5**. Rows 1 and 2 are never once placed on
target in five attempts; rows 3–5 are placed on target in three or four of five. That is a
difference in kind and it does not vanish with the gate verdict.

A sample-level Fisher exact on rows 1+2 (0 of 10) against rows 3+4+5 (11 of 15) gives
**p = 0.000545**. 🔴 **That number should not be believed and is recorded here so nobody computes it
later and believes it.** The five samples of one design are not five independent observations of
that design — they are repeat draws on one input, so the test is pseudo-replicated and its n is
inflated. The honest unit of analysis is the design, which gives n = 2 against n = 3 and no usable
test. So the contrast is reported **descriptively**: Chai-1 never puts rows 1 and 2 on target, and
usually puts rows 3–5 there. No gate verdict, no p-value, no claim that rows 1 and 2 are misplaced.

## 🔴 D8b also failed, on one row

Sample 0 was pre-registered to reproduce C4's single draw within 0.5 Å. Five of six did, to between
0.002 and 0.076 Å. **Row 6 drifted 1.968 Å** (34.693 against 36.661), so the check FAILS as written.

The explanation — flagged as an explanation, not as a pass — is that row 6 is the unstable design in
the set, with a sample range spanning 1.66 to 34.69 Å. A molecule sitting on a bifurcation is
exactly where an otherwise-deterministic draw stops reproducing. Five rows matching to under 0.08 Å
shows the pipeline itself is deterministic, so this is not a wiring fault. But a pre-registered check
that fails is a check that failed, and it is recorded as one.

## Consequences

1. **The "three of six fail the second oracle" claim is withdrawn** from the submission and replaced
   with what D8 actually supports: a single Chai-1 draw is not a usable pose check on this system,
   and our rescore measured that rather than the designs.
2. **The ranking still does not change** — it never depended on this, and now there is even less
   reason to touch it.
3. **The missing-oracle disclosure stands unchanged.** The pre-registration was still breached, the
   designs are still round-4 descendants, and the pose claims are still exploratory. D8 removes a
   piece of *evidence*, not the admission.
4. **It does not revive the corrective rounds re-run.** That stopped on cost, and 5 samples per fold
   makes the per-fold cost worse.
